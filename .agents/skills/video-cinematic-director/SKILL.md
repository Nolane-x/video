# Cinematic Video Director

## Story-first preproduction
1. Define audience, platform, premise, emotional promise, duration, language and constraints from the known brief.
2. Create a logline, beginning/middle/end, turning points, emotional arc and specific ending image.
3. Build a shot list where every shot changes action, space, information, relationship, emotion, scale or visual metaphor.
4. For every shot record: purpose, duration, framing, angle, camera rationale, environment, props, actor action, anticipation/contact/follow-through/recovery, on-screen text, subtitle line, transition and sound beat.
5. Create an art bible: character silhouette/proportions, palette, material response, practical-light logic, texture, environment and typography.

## Visual direction
Preserve the current cinematic, story-led stick-figure direction: simple repeatable symbolic characters staged in rich environments; motivated camera; readable acting and prop contact; foreground/background depth; restrained practical light. Do not regress to repeated flat SVG cards, generic gradients, pseudo-real AI humans or empty backgrounds.

## Motion and acting
Build a clear silhouette before detail. Show anticipation → action → contact/impact → follow-through → recovery. Animate prop and actor as one interaction. Vary wide, medium, close, insert, over-shoulder, reaction and establishing shots. Camera movement must reveal, follow or emphasize action, not disguise a static scene.

## Reference discipline
Analyze public references at timestamps and record observable framing/action/light/sound. Do not copy characters, assets, dialogue, scripts or music. Record uncertainty where the source was not actually watched.

## Deliverables
Logline, beat sheet, storyboard/animatic plan, character/environment style bible, shot JSON and a risk list for difficult actions.
