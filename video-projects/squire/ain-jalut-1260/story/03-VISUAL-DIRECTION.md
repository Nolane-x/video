# Visual direction — The Day the Mongols Were Stopped at Ain Jalut

## Sequence blocks (paragraph IDs refer to 01-SCRIPT.md; final seconds come from the VoiceStudio timeline)
1. **P001–P003 — Cold open in the arrow storm:** 3–5 quick cuts; close shield, incoming arrows, horse pivot, compressed Mamluk line, distant mounted silhouettes. Start at danger, no title card before the action.
2. **P004–P006 — Baghdad, 1258:** establish the Tigris, walls and city gates; interior scholarly/market/workshop inserts; restrained siege panorama; aftermath shown without gore or invented casualty counts.
3. **P007–P009 — Syria opens:** parchment route from Baghdad to Aleppo and Damascus; maps progressively reveal territory, not an instant omniscient view.
4. **P010–P012 — The succession crisis:** Mongol command tent, messenger and route withdrawing east; show the western detachment remaining as a labelled formation, not exact troop arithmetic.
5. **P013–P016 — Cairo and the Mamluk army:** command tent, armour and saddle preparation, horse care, Qutuz and Baybars as unnamed generic stylised commanders with name cards only, training and formation cohesion.
6. **P017–P020 — The road north:** route map Egypt → Gaza → Acre / coastal corridor → Jezreel Valley; scouts, supply animals, water stops, mounted vanguard and terrain inserts.
7. **P021–P024 — Two mounted systems:** Mongol horse archer bow / quiver / release inserts; Mamluk armour and lance; compare mobility and protection without simplistic “fast versus slow” stereotypes.
8. **P025–P027 — The battle:** build escalation over multiple shots: mounted archery, line under pressure, uncertain withdrawal, close combat, reserve commitment, Qutuz rally, counterattack, Kitbuqa's death and pursuit. Intercut wides, close reactions and overhead map diagrams. Never assert the feigned retreat as settled fact.
9. **P028–P030 — Victory and the unfinished war:** quieter field, broken standards, retreat route, map showing later continuing frontier pressure, Qutuz's assassination and Baybars's accession, then final visual reversal: the “unstoppable” army was vulnerable under a specific set of conditions.

## Shot vocabulary
- scene: lived environment, camp, siege, march, cavalry movement and field action.
- close: YOU, commanders, hands, armour, reins, bowstring and reactions.
- army: large mounted formations and marching bodies.
- top / plan: explanatory battlefield geometry, cavalry routes and reserve movement.
- map: Baghdad / Syria / Egypt / Gaza / Acre / Ayn Jalut / Cairo context and route.
- item: arrowheads, composite bow, mail, helmet, saddle, reins, water skins, standards.
- split / board: compare mounted roles, equipment and campaign positions without unsourced numerical bars.
- title: cold open should not use a title; use a later short location/date card after the viewer is oriented.

## Specific choreography
- Arrow beat: close-up nock → release → flight → shield/ground contact → reaction. Do not show arrows passing through armour by default.
- Horse beat: horse gait and rider move together; use staggered riders, not a single static horse parade.
- Feigned-retreat beat: render as competing explanatory diagrams. Show one version as “one account says” and contrast with an alternative reconstruction; don't lock a single route in as historical certainty.
- Counterattack: show reserves committing, line regaining cohesion and a controlled forward push; avoid an implausible perfect circular trap.
- Aftermath: sober, non-graphic; no piles of bodies or triumphant celebration over casualties.

## Maps and labels
- Main map: Cairo, Gaza, Acre, Damascus, Aleppo, Baghdad and approximate Ayn Jalut location.
- Labels: MAMLUKS, MONGOLS, BAGHDAD, CAIRO, GAZA, ACRE, AYN JALUT, 1260.
- Map and troop positions are explanatory; no coordinate-precision claim.

## Audio requirements
- Use recorded CC0 horse-gallop, crowd, wind and weapon/arrow material from the curated Foley acquisition sources where available.
- Use score beds only where they support the narration; duck music beneath speech, avoid continuous SFX carpets.
- Put a short silence or near-silence pocket at the moment the tide turns and at the final line.
- Generate SRT/VTT sidecars from the verified voice track; keep on-screen text to readable, short labels.
