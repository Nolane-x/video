# Ain Jalut — visual and historical bible

## Project identity
- Working title: The Day the Mongols Were Stopped at Ain Jalut
- Standalone H2 Squire long-form history film. This is not a Cannae sequel or a recut.
- 16:9, 1920×1080, 24 fps, English narration.
- Squire vector/cel-shaded look: white round-headed characters, dark brown outlines, tactile props, layered landscapes, moving camera, dust, banners, horses and readable tactical graphics.
- No photoreal faces, no AI-image inserts, no faux-archival imagery, no gore.

## Point of view
The viewer follows a Mamluk cavalryman as a visual witness, not as a documented named historical person. Keep this "YOU" character visually consistent: turban-wrapped helmet silhouette, mail or layered armour, deep blue-grey cloak, round shield, lance or sword, chestnut/bay horse. His equipment is an illustrative composite, not a claim of one standard Mamluk uniform.

## Factions and visual shorthand
- Mamluk force: deep blue / blue-grey (#41677f), muted cream and brass details; use round shields, lances, swords and selected mounted archer imagery.
- Mongol force: warm rust / burnt sienna (#a6533b), dark leather and muted ochre; show mounted archers, composite bows, quivers, light horse tack and fast horse movement.
- Use the Squire greek, knight12, latinOld and englishArcher presets only as generic rig/shape sources. Override costume colours, helmets, shields and weapons wherever an individual character is authored. Do not depict the Mongols as English archers or the Mamluks as Greek hoplites in the narrative; they are engine placeholders.
- Formation crowd presets cannot express all the historical costume detail. Label tactical diagrams by side and role, not by pretending the generic crowd figures are exact archaeological reconstructions.

## Visual grammar
- Cold open: close-up shield and incoming arrow, dust and a horse turning; reveal only fragments of the field.
- Baghdad: broad city wall / river / gate imagery, then quieter domestic and scholarly inserts; do not invent exact city architecture as a surveyed reconstruction.
- Mongol advance: parchment map from the Iranian plateau through Baghdad and Syria; red route grows only as narration reaches each place.
- The succession crisis: map route and campaign line pauses; visualise the westward army thinning, not a literal numerical headcount.
- Cairo and Mamluk state: command tent, armour/horse prep, scrolls, horse lines, close-up of Qutuz and Baybars as stylised generic commanders, never likenesses.
- March north: map route, supply loads, water, horse lines, scouts, Gaza, Acre and the Jezreel Valley.
- Battlefield: alternate mounted action, bow / arrow inserts, medium cavalry ranks, overhead tactical diagrams, reaction close-ups and environmental wides.
- Aftermath: reduce motion, leave abandoned standards and the field, then shift to a political map of the continuing frontier.
- End on the contrast between a reputation of invincibility and a force that could be defeated under the right conditions.

## Motion standards
- No universal zoom-only animation. Change shot size, angle, subject and camera path.
- Action beats show anticipation, release, travel, contact, reaction and recovery.
- Horse gaits must use the horse rig (gallop / walk) with the rider attached.
- Arrows must travel through space and resolve at a visible target; never show impacts without a corresponding projectile or contact beat.
- Formation diagrams are schematic and labelled. Distinguish flanking pressure from a complete encirclement; do not draw a perfect circle unless the narrated source supports it.
- Use no more than three successive shots with the same composition or template.
- Long-form narration is not burned in as fragmented captions. Deliver clean SRT and VTT sidecars; on-screen typography is limited to maps, short name/location cards and tactical labels.

## Audio arc
- Opening: real low-level battlefield ambience and close hoof / arrow detail under narration.
- Baghdad / campaign: restrained wind, city bed and a sparse score; avoid continuous battle SFX.
- Mamluk preparation: tack, armour handling, horse breath and a quiet pulse.
- Battle: recorded horse gallop, recorded crowd layer, selected weapon contact and arrow passes; use only at visible sync points.
- Aftermath: remove percussion, let space and a restrained low score carry the scene.
- Closing: calm, unresolved historical motif; no triumphant victory anthem.
- Voice remains intelligible; mix target around −14 LUFS and true peak no higher than −1 dBTP. Every recorded sample's creator, item licence, source URL and SHA-256 must be retained. Machine decoding is not human listening approval.

## Historical guardrails
- Main date: 3 September 1260, often given as 25 Ramadan 658 AH. Use the Gregorian date in narration.
- The Mamluk army was led by Sultan Qutuz; Baybars commanded an important vanguard role. The Mongol force in Syria was commanded by Kitbuqa after Hülegü withdrew east following Möngke's death.
- The battle checked the Mongol advance into Egypt; it did not end Mongol power or all later Ilkhanid campaigns.
- Do not present medieval casualty or army totals as exact modern counts. Avoid spectacular numbers.
- Sources disagree about the precise battle sequence and the role of a feigned retreat. State the disagreement clearly.
- Generic Squire presets are stylised illustration assets, not authenticated costumes.
