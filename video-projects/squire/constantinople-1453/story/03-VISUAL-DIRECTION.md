# Visual direction — 53 Days to the End of an Empire

## Picture grammar
Not a lecture made from slides. Treat the viewer as physically present in the city. Use layered foreground debris, mid-ground actors and deep city/harbour geography. Move props and characters independently; camera motion must follow, reveal or react to action.

## Visual chapters
1. Final night: torchlight, exhausted defenders, silent artillery, distant army. Fast sensory fragments, then hard cut to the first day.
2. The city: elevated panorama of the Bosphorus, Golden Horn, land walls, towers and domed skyline. Map inserts only when geography is genuinely being explained.
3. The sultan's preparation: Rumeli Hisarı, the strait, gun casting/loading, artillery convoy, command tent, crews and oxen.
4. The bombardment: a complete cause/effect chain—powder, loading, match, recoil, muzzle flash, smoke, stone ball flight, impact, debris, defender reaction, repair crew.
5. The sea: chain across the Golden Horn, four relief ships, moving galleys, fire and spray, ships entering harbour, defenders turning their attention.
6. The overland ships: hulls on rollers, ropes under tension, men hauling, wheels/rollers moving, slope, distant skyline. Use careful label: “reported operation; exact route debated.”
7. The attrition: tunnel/countermine, repair crews, supply scarcity, harbour guard, civilians, prayer, wounded and command decisions.
8. Final night and assault: no static tableau. Distinct waves approach; ladders rise, attackers climb, defenders push back, arrows cross, torches flicker, smoke drifts, commands pass, Giustiniani is carried away, line breaks.
9. Aftermath: silence, smoke, damaged walls, people moving through a changed city. End with the city's continued life, not an empty ruin.

## Shot requirements
- 550–800 shots expected for the long-form timeline; do not pad with random cuts.
- Typical shot length 0.9–2.2 seconds, with longer 3–4 second establishing or emotional holds.
- Every shot changes at least one meaningful property: action, camera, location, scale, information, emotional state or sound perspective.
- Maintain a readable visual anchor. Do not cut so quickly that a key action cannot be understood.
- Use wide, low-angle, high-angle, over-shoulder, macro insert, reaction, tracking and overhead compositions.
- Camera movement is separate from subject motion.
- Keep title cards short; put historical names/dates on screen only when the narration introduces them.

## 240 fps choreography
- Every custom motion path is evaluated from elapsed seconds, with a deterministic seed.
- No wall-clock time or unseeded random animation.
- 240 fps is real native rendering. The animation curves remain continuous; we are not creating 240 by repeating 24 or 30 fps frames.
- Small secondary motion—cloth, flags, ropes, water, smoke, dust, fire, debris—is layered behind the primary action, never used to obscure it.

## Safe areas
Captions remain in the lower safe area and never cover the main face, weapon contact, ladder top or cannonball impact. Use concise, readable line breaks. Keep labels separate from narration captions.

## Historical caution
A siege reconstruction can show plausible spatial relationships but is not a literal reconstruction of every movement. Do not add exact troop counts, exact cannon dimensions or invented quotes unless the research ledger supports them.
