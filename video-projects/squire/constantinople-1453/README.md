# 53 Days to the End of an Empire

A standalone English-language historical animation about the siege and fall of Constantinople, 6 April–29 May 1453.

## Master target
- H2 Squire, 1920×1080, **native 240 fps**.
- The 240 fps master is rendered from 240 distinct time samples per second. No duplicated-frame inflation and no frame-rate metadata trick.
- H.264 high-bitrate master, 48 Mbps VBV ceiling, CRF 18, slow preset.
- Full-length story with word-aligned captions, a dense but readable shot rhythm, purpose-built siege sequences and bespoke multi-channel motion.

## Visual approach
Keep the established Squire historical-animation grammar, but stop treating the entire film as a sequence of generic template swaps. Custom shots handle the Theodosian walls, artillery impacts, the Golden Horn naval breakthrough, the overland ship operation and the final assault. Each of those sequences has moving subjects, camera, depth layers, environmental motion and cause/effect beats.

## Story approach
A fictional composite defender anchors the story without being presented as a documented historical person. The narration opens on the final night, rewinds to 6 April, and builds through five escalating problems: the guns, the sea blockade, the ships inside the Golden Horn, the defenders' exhaustion, and the final assault. Contested details are flagged rather than converted into certainty.

## Rendering and review
1. Select a currently eligible English VoiceStudio narrator and render the full narration.
2. Align the voice, generate timed shots and author custom visual sequences.
3. Inspect the 240 fps moving proof before the full master.
4. Render every frame natively at 240 fps.
5. Run technical QA, including exact ffprobe rate and decoded frame count.
6. Human full-length watch/listen remains a separate artistic approval gate. Technical success and contact sheets are not artistic sign-off.

## Important
240 fps is the delivered master rate, not the number of frames the renderer can calculate per second. A normal 60 Hz display will show at most 60 unique refreshes per second; the higher-rate master preserves denser temporal sampling for compatible playback and slow-motion editorial use.
