# Visual direction — The Roman Consul Who Rode Into Hannibal's Trap

## Format and style
- Short, vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920, 24 fps, English narration.
- H2 Squire vector animation: white round-headed figures, dark brown outlines, flat cel shading, soft layered battlefield depth, moving camera, hand-drawn parchment map and overhead tactical diagrams.
- Fast opening, clear visual cause-and-effect, varied close / army / overhead / map compositions. Keep captions in the lower safe band and never place an important face, weapon, formation or label behind them.
- Paullus is the recurring visual anchor. Do not depict real-person likenesses.

## Historical guardrails
- Date: 2 August 216 BCE. Setting: Cannae in Apulia, beside the River Aufidus.
- Paullus was consul with Varro. Polybius places him on the Roman right, where he survived the cavalry engagement. Seeing the decisive action developing among the infantry, he rode to the centre; Polybius also places Hannibal there. Carthaginian cavalry then struck the Roman rear. Paullus fell in the fighting, covered with wounds.
- Show the encirclement as a simplified explanatory diagram, not an exact minute-by-minute map or surveyed reconstruction. Do not display disputed force totals or casualty estimates.
- Faction palette is a storytelling aid only: Roman formations in deep red with muted gold; Carthaginian formations in blue. Do not imply that every Carthaginian contingent wore a standardized blue uniform or shield.

## Beat design
1. Start inside the cavalry clash: moving horse, impact, dust, immediate scale.
2. Reveal Paullus in red consul's cloak; use a readable PAULLUS name sign.
3. Short date card, then a parchment map of central/southern Italy, showing Rome, Cannae and the Aufidus.
4. Establish Paullus on the Roman right with mounted action, then pivot to the infantry centre.
5. Use a marching Roman army shot and a clear overhead formation diagram: Roman pressure advances while the Carthaginian centre yields.
6. Make the two inward-turning wings unmistakable in a top-down tactical diagram. Highlight exposed Roman flanks.
7. Return to Paullus: a brief choice beat, then a mounted ride toward the centre. Do not portray him as intentionally choosing death; the line is a narrative hook, not a claim about his private thoughts.
8. Give Hannibal one concise blue-toned close shot; show both commanders in the central melee.
9. Rear cavalry arrives. The overhead formation closes around the compressed Roman mass.
10. Depict Paullus's final moments non-graphically. Brief music drop at his fall; no gore.
11. Close on the tightening tactical diagram and the final line.
