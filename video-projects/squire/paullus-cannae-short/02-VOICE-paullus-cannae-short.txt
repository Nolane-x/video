August second, two hundred and sixteen BCE. |
Near Cannae, Rome faces Hannibal's army. |
Lucius Aemilius Paullus is one of Rome's two consuls. |
Polybius places him on Rome's right. |
But the decisive struggle is forming in the centre. |
Roman infantry pushes forward. |
Hannibal's centre gives ground. |
The Romans surge deeper. |
Carthaginian infantry turns inward from both wings. |
Rome's flanks are exposed. |
Paullus could remain on the right. |
Instead, he rides toward the infantry. |
The battle will be decided there. |
Hannibal is fighting in the centre too. |
Then Carthaginian cavalry strikes the Roman rear. |
The legions are trapped in their own packed ranks. |
As the ranks fall, the Roman circle contracts. |
Paullus falls in the thick of the fighting, covered with wounds. |
He did not ride toward safety. |
He rode toward the point where Rome needed him most. |
Hannibal's trap was closing around him.
