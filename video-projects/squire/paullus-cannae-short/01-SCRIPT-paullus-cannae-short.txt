A brutal cavalry fight is raging. One Roman consul survives it. Then he rides toward the centre as Hannibal's trap closes.

August second, two hundred and sixteen BCE. Near Cannae in southern Italy, Rome faces Hannibal's army. Lucius Aemilius Paullus is one of Rome's two consuls. Polybius places him on the Roman right. He survives the cavalry clash.

But the decision is forming in the centre. Roman infantry pushes forward. Hannibal's centre gives ground. The Romans surge deeper into a narrowing space. Then Carthaginian infantry on both wings turns inward. The Roman flanks are exposed.

Paullus could remain on the right. Instead, he rides toward the infantry. The battle will be decided there. Polybius says Hannibal is fighting in the centre too. Both commanders are inside the same tightening battle.

Then Carthaginian cavalry reaches the Roman rear. Around them, the Roman formation keeps shrinking. The legions are trapped in their own crowded formation. Paullus falls in the thick of the fighting, covered with wounds.

He did not ride toward safety. He rode toward the point where Rome needed him most. That was exactly where Hannibal's trap was closing.
