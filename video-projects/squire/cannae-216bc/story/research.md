# Cannae — research and historical production bible

## Core thesis
The Roman infantry mass advanced into a projecting Carthaginian centre that yielded ground. The advance exposed both Roman flanks to Carthaginian Libyan infantry; Carthaginian cavalry superiority helped complete the encirclement. The film explains a tactical reconstruction, not an exact frame-by-frame record.

## Source hierarchy and facts used
1. **Polybius, Histories 3.107–118** — primary ancient narrative, translated at the University of Chicago's LacusCurtius / Penelope site: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/3%2A.html
   - 3.107: Hannibal seized Cannae's citadel and Roman supply stores.
   - 3.113: Roman formation deeper than usual; about 80,000 infantry and slightly more than 6,000 cavalry; Carthaginian force about 40,000 infantry and 10,000 cavalry; crescent-shaped Iberian/Gallic centre; African infantry on the wings; cavalry on both wings.
   - 3.115–116: cavalry action, Roman advance into the centre, African infantry attacking both flanks, encirclement and death of Paullus.
   - 3.117: Polybius gives about 70,000 Roman dead; explicitly treated in the script as an ancient estimate, not a modern verified count.
2. **Polybius, Book III 107–118, Fordham Ancient History Sourcebook**: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/ancient/polybius-cannae.asp
   - Parallel translation and accessible passages for the supply issue, force composition and battle phases.
3. **Polybius 3.113.1–3.113.9, Humanitext Reader**: https://reader.humanitext.ai/en/read/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0543.tlg001.humanitext-grc2%3A3.113.1-3.113.9
   - Text and translation notes for Roman deployment depth and the crescent centre. The translation page flags its English translation as machine-generated; use it only as a cross-check, not the controlling translation.
4. **Ancient battle studies (archive.org scan hosted by Wikimedia Commons)**: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/Battle_studies_-_ancient_and_modern_battle_%28IA_battlestudiesanc00ardaiala%29.pdf
   - Later tactical analysis of the battle; use only as secondary interpretation, not as primary evidence.

## Historical caution
- Ancient troop strengths and casualty totals vary by author and are not modern audited counts. The voiceover attributes its headline strengths to Polybius and uses approximations.
- The exact minute-by-minute cavalry movement and the precise choreography of the encirclement are reconstructed. Avoid claiming that the ancient sources provide a modern, fully synchronized operations map.
- The film uses an illustrative vector battlefield. The Squire pack's ancient presets are generic stylized soldiers, not authenticated reconstructions of Punic, Iberian, Gallic or Roman equipment.
- Do not invent direct quotations, dialogue, banners, unit names or personal biographies.
- Map geography is approximate: Cannae lies in Apulia near the Aufidus (modern Ofanto) River. The map is a visual locator, not a survey.

## Visual and sound rules
- Roman force: muted rust / brick red blocks and flags. Carthaginian force: blue-grey / indigo blocks.
- Use generic Squire ancient characters with consistent palette; avoid implying that latin or hastatus is a historically exact Carthaginian soldier.
- The visual grammar alternates: geography map → character reaction → force-size comparison → battle line → top-down movement → close combat → aftermath map → strategic consequence.
- No gore, graphic injuries, bodies as decoration, or triumphant celebration over casualties.
- Narrator: one consistent English voice, exact VoiceStudio manifest ID saved in the voice-selection JSON; no celebrity imitation.
- Sound arc: low ancient tension, measured drums as forces deploy, cavalry/metal/hoof SFX during the opening, muted pulse during the centre's retreat, dense but restrained battle impacts during encirclement, then silence/low somber bed for the aftermath and strategic conclusion.
