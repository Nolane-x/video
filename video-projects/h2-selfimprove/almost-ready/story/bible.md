# ALMOST READY — character, camera and art bible

## Story
A student/young artist is preparing a portfolio for a time-limited submission. Each revision feels safer than letting someone judge the work. The application closes before he sends anything. A friend asks to see the project; he shares an imperfect version and discovers that being seen is survivable. The ending does not claim that craft or revision is bad: the change is from endless polishing to purposeful finishing and feedback.

## Locked brief
- Target runtime: 172 seconds, 29 shots, 16:9, 1920×1080 at 24 fps.
- Language: English on-screen text. No voice-over.
- Engine: native H2 SelfImprove Studio Canvas/Chromium plus FFmpeg audio mastering.
- Accent: amber used sparingly for the temptation voice, deadline/count emphasis, practical lamps and end-card accent.
- Palette: charcoal/plum room shadows; cool blue screen light during avoidance; amber practical light and sunrise during agency and reconnection.

## Character continuity
- Hero: same cream round-headed young man throughout, charcoal hoodie in avoidance/night scenes, plain outfit in the recovery sequence. No redesign between shots.
- Mina: established girl character preset with a warm yellow scarf, attentive but not theatrical; she is a friend, not a magical rescuer.
- Voice: tall dark shadow with restrained amber rim and a soft grin. It is physically close and visually large in avoidance; much smaller at b27. No supernatural effects except a slight restrained rim glow.
- Face acting: pride/curiosity at the first draft; worry when he spots a flaw; avoidance during the deadline; shame at the closed portal; cautious attention while sharing; relief rather than triumph when Mina responds.

## Sets and staging
- Bedroom studio: recurring anchor set with bed kept to the left, desk/laptop to the right, window above/back, warm desk lamp. Use stage=true so architecture/edges, furniture and window dressing provide depth.
- Classroom/studio: desks, board and a visible wall surface; show other people sharing work while the hero stays slightly behind.
- Phone/laptop inserts: the device must tell the story with legible UI; use phone for private messages/feed and laptop for deadlines/email. Keep captions away from the device UI.
- Void: only for the deadline collapse, a controlled spotlight with the hero and shadow in distinct positions.
- Café: rainy window, hanging amber lamp, table and two seated characters with clear eye-line across the table.
- City: dawn tracking shot should be motivated by a walk and changing architecture, not a zoom-only transition.

## Camera and performance
- Alternate wide/medium/insert/face/over-shoulder/two-shot. No identical centered layouts for more than two consecutive shots.
- Push-in only to underline a readable action. Walking, reaching, writing, typing and the phone/laptop events should happen in the actor/prop, not only in camera movement.
- For each key action inspect anticipation, action, contact, follow-through and recovery: pen-to-page at b03/b20; clock transition at b13; typed/sent action b21–b22; phone response b23; eye-line across the café table b24.
- Avoid giant title cards apart from the intentional black hook/collapse/end card. Do not put captions over faces, hands or key UI text.

## Audio
- Sad sparse piano at the start; restrained dark texture through revision/avoidance; complete silence at the deadline/collapse; a quieter hopeful harmonic shift at the human message; pulse/rise around sending; warm resolve at the ending.
- Use only SFX names shipped by the H2 sound bank: page turns, deadline ticks, notification, send confirmation and footsteps. Effects are consequence-driven; avoid constant whooshes or SFX masking captions.

## Acceptance gates
- Schema and duration pass; all 29 shots represented with zero timeline gaps.
- Contact sheets cover every shot; verify typography, actor scale, face, set geography and continuity.
- Inspect at least 12 motion proofs, especially pen contact, deadline clock, closed portal, sharing action, phone reply, two-person café staging and the shadow retreat.
- FFprobe and EBU R128 are run on the exact mastered MP4. Target −14 LUFS ±1, true peak ≤ −1 dBFS.
- A successful render alone is not artistic sign-off. Watch the complete export with audio before final acceptance.
