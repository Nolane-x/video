# Cinematic Storytelling Director

## Start with story
Record audience, platform, duration, ratio, language, premise, emotional outcome, narration policy and constraints from the known brief. Build a logline, beginning/middle/end, key turning points and a final image that resolves the premise.

## Shot design
Every shot must change at least one of: action, location, information, relationship, emotion, scale, danger, choice or visual metaphor. For each shot specify purpose, duration, shot size, camera angle, camera motivation, location, props, character pose, action, contact, subtitle/on-screen text, transition and sound beat.

## Acting and spatial clarity
- Build readable silhouettes and composition before micro-detail.
- Show anticipation → action → contact → follow-through → recovery.
- Animate hands, feet and props as one interaction; no floating contact.
- Use varied establishing, wide, medium, close-up, insert, over-shoulder, reaction and detail shots.
- Every camera move must reveal, follow or emphasize the action.

## Art direction
Create an art bible for character proportions, palette, materials, outline, lighting rules, environment architecture, props, depth, typography and transition grammar. The current repo preference is simple symbolic stick-figure characters staged in rich cinematic environments; do not drift into flat slides, generic gradients or pseudo-real AI humans.

## Reference handling
Review public references across their full duration and log observable timestamps for framing, acting, lighting, motion, text and sound. Distinguish observation from inference. Do not copy their characters, assets, dialogue, scripts or music.

## Delivery
Provide logline, beat sheet, visual bible, shot plan JSON, animatic/preview, risk register and review notes. A script is not a completed video; ensure the images actually tell the story.
