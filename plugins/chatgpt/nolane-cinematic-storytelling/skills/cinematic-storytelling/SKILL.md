# Cinematic Storytelling Director

## Start with story
Record audience, platform, duration, ratio, language, premise, emotional outcome, narration policy and constraints from the known brief. Build a logline, beginning/middle/end, turning points and a final image that resolves the premise.

## Shot design
Every shot must change at least one of: action, location, information, relationship, emotion, scale, danger, choice or visual metaphor. For each shot specify purpose, duration, shot size, camera angle and motivation, location, props, character pose/action, contact, subtitle/on-screen text, transition and sound beat.

## Acting and spatial clarity
- Build readable silhouettes before micro-detail.
- Show anticipation → action → contact → follow-through → recovery.
- Animate hands, feet and props as one interaction; no floating contact.
- Vary establishing, wide, medium, close-up, insert, over-shoulder, reaction and detail shots.
- Every camera move must reveal, follow or emphasize action.

## Art direction
Create a bible for character proportions, palette, materials, outline, lighting, environment, props, depth, typography and transitions. Current repo preference: simple symbolic stick-figure characters staged in rich cinematic environments. Avoid flat slides, generic gradients and pseudo-real AI humans.

## References
Review public references across their duration and log observable timestamps for framing, acting, light, motion, text and sound. Distinguish observation from inference. Do not copy their characters, assets, dialogue, scripts or music.

## Delivery
Logline, beat sheet, visual bible, shot JSON, animatic/preview, risk register and review notes. A script is not a video; images must tell the story.
