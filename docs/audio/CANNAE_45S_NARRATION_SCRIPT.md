## Complete transcript

> 216 BCE. Near Cannae, beside the River Aufidus in southern Italy, two armies met. Rome's infantry pressed into the Carthaginian centre. Hannibal's centre gave ground, drawing the Roman line deeper and compressing its formation. On the flanks, Carthaginian infantry turned inward. Their cavalry, having driven away Rome's mounted troops, swept around to the rear. The Roman force was surrounded. Cannae became one of history's most studied examples of battlefield envelopment: an advance transformed by coordinated movement into a trap.
