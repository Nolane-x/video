# Cinematic Video Director

## Story-first preproduction
Define audience, platform, premise, emotional promise, duration, language, source evidence and constraints. Build a logline, beginning/middle/end, turning points, emotional arc and specific ending image. Plan the paper edit and radio edit before locking picture timing.

## Choose a visual grammar, do not impose one
Preserve the established cinematic stick-figure style only when that series/style is selected. For footage-led or high-motion history, use the historical-cinematic-documentary-director route. Visual media may include archival footage, museum objects, real photography, painted art, layered 2.5D plates, Blender 3D reconstructions, Three.js animation, maps and authored motion graphics. Squire is not the universal history renderer.

## Shot blueprint
Every shot has: purpose, duration, framing/scale, angle/height, lens intention, camera path and destination, speed/easing, focus plane, screen direction, subject action, prop interaction, depth layers, light/materials, narration phrase, on-screen text, transition reason and sound beat.

## Motion and acting
Build a readable silhouette and staging before detail. Show anticipation → action → contact → reaction → follow-through → recovery. Animate props and actors as one physical interaction. The camera must reveal, follow or emphasize the action—not disguise a static scene. Use tracking, orbit, crane, dolly, parallax, rack focus, pan/tilt, whip, aerial and holds where motivated. Vary scale and angle across sequences; avoid repeated faces, weapon inserts, crowd rows or generic diagrams.

## Frame-seek safety
All shot motion must render deterministically from the requested frame/time. Avoid wall-clock timers and stateful simulation in render paths. Test random seek order, temporal seams, object trajectories and contact frames.

## Sound integration
Every shot must have an audio intention: ambience, Foley, score, transition, silence or deliberate none. Search and audition real licensed recordings in the existing audio library first. Do not assume generated/synthesized sounds will pass as Foley.

## Reference discipline
Analyze supplied public references at timestamps and record observable framing/action/light/sound. Do not copy characters, assets, dialogue, scripts, music or proprietary compositions. Record uncertainty when the source could not be watched.

## Deliverables
Story and evidence map, radio edit, art bible, shot JSON, camera/action diagrams, resource/rights manifest, sound cue map and risk list for difficult sequences.
